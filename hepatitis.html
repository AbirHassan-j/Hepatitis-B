What is Hepatitis B?

Hepatitis B is an infection caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV). It mainly affects the liver.

HBV

A cartoon-style virus model, not to scale.

How does it affect liver cells?

  1. The virus reaches the liver.

    HBV can enter the body through infected blood or certain body fluids.

  2. It infects liver cells.

    The virus brings genetic instructions into a liver cell and uses the cell's machinery to make more copies.

  3. The immune system responds.

    Immune cells try to control the infection. This response can also injure infected liver cells.

  4. Long-lasting infection can cause damage.

    Chronic hepatitis B can lead to repeated inflammation, scarring (fibrosis), and sometimes cirrhosis over time.

Can Hepatitis B be prevented?

Yes. Vaccination is a very effective way to prevent Hepatitis B. Testing and medical care can also help people manage the infection. The virus is not spread by hugging, sharing food, or casual everyday contact.

This page is for learning, not for diagnosing or treating illness.
